# Anti-Harassment, Equity & Diversity

## Why this matters

Everyone — customers and colleagues — has the right to be treated with dignity. This module covers what harassment and discrimination look like in a retail/store environment, and what to do if you experience or witness it.

## What discrimination looks like

Treating someone less favorably because of a protected characteristic — race, gender, age, disability, religion, sexual orientation, pregnancy, or similar — in things like:

- Scheduling (always giving someone the worst shifts)
- Task assignment (always giving someone the "bad" jobs)
- Hiring, promotion, or discipline decisions
- How customers are served or spoken to

## What harassment looks like

Unwanted conduct related to a protected characteristic, or of a sexual nature, that violates someone's dignity or creates an intimidating, hostile, or offensive environment. Examples:

- Jokes, "banter," or comments about someone's appearance, accent, religion, or background that they haven't invited
- Unwanted physical contact, comments about someone's body, or repeated requests for dates after being declined
- Name-calling or exclusion based on a protected characteristic
- Sharing offensive images or messages in a work group chat

Intent doesn't erase impact — "I was only joking" is not a defense if the conduct was unwanted and would reasonably be seen as offensive.

## Customer conduct

Customers don't get a pass either. If a customer harasses or discriminates against you or a colleague, you're entitled to support — tell your manager. You should never be expected to simply "take it" to avoid losing a sale.

## What to do

- **If it's happening to you:** tell the person (if you feel safe doing so) that the behavior is unwelcome, and report it to your manager or the designated contact regardless.
- **If you witness it happening to someone else:** don't stay silent. Check in with the person and report what you saw.
- **If you're not sure whether something crossed a line:** ask. A conversation with your manager costs nothing; letting a pattern continue unchecked costs a lot.

Reports are taken seriously, investigated fairly, and retaliation against anyone who reports in good faith is itself a violation of this policy.

## Building an inclusive team day to day

- Assume good intent but listen when someone tells you something landed badly — adjust rather than argue.
- Rotate the undesirable tasks and shifts fairly across the team.
- Make space for different working styles and backgrounds rather than expecting everyone to fit one mold.
