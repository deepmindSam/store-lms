# Conflict of Interest & Anti-Bribery

## What is a conflict of interest?

A conflict of interest happens when your personal interests (money, relationships, favors) could influence — or appear to influence — the decisions you make at work. It doesn't require you to actually act dishonestly; even the *appearance* of a conflict can damage trust.

## Common examples in a store environment

- **Hiring or supervising a relative or close friend** without disclosing the relationship.
- **Accepting gifts or discounts from suppliers** in exchange for favorable treatment (bigger orders, better shelf space, overlooking quality issues).
- **Approving your own expenses, discounts, or returns.**
- **Working a second job for a competitor or supplier** without telling your employer.
- **Steering business toward a company owned by a friend or family member.**

## The anti-bribery rule, simply put

Never give, offer, or accept anything of value in exchange for favorable treatment in business decisions. This includes cash, gifts, "loans," excessive hospitality, or favors. A small, transparent gift at a holiday (clearly disclosed, within normal business courtesy) is usually fine; anything that feels like it's trying to influence a decision, or that you'd be uncomfortable disclosing to your manager, is not.

## What you need to do

1. **Disclose, don't hide.** If you have a personal relationship with a supplier, a relative working at the same store, or any other situation that could create a conflict, tell your manager so it can be managed openly.
2. **Never approve your own transactions.** Returns, discounts, and expense claims involving you personally should always be approved by someone else.
3. **Decline gifts or hospitality that could look like a bribe.** If in doubt, ask your manager before accepting anything from a supplier, contractor, or customer seeking special treatment.
4. **Keep business decisions on business merits.** Choose suppliers, vendors, and partners based on price, quality, and reliability — not personal relationships.

## A simple test

Before accepting a gift, hiring a friend, or making an exception for someone — ask: *"Would I be comfortable if my manager, or a customer, saw exactly what I'm about to do?"* If the honest answer is no, don't do it, and talk to your manager instead.
