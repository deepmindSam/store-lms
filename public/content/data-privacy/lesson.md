# Data Privacy

## Why this matters

Every day we handle personal information: customer names, phone numbers, addresses, payment details, loyalty account data, even CCTV footage. Mishandling this data can harm real people — enabling fraud, stalking, or identity theft — and can expose the business to fines and loss of customer trust. This isn't a legal technicality; it's part of treating customers and colleagues with respect.

## What counts as personal data

Any information that identifies, or could reasonably be used to identify, a person:

- Name, address, phone number, email address
- Payment card details, bank details
- ID/passport numbers, date of birth
- Purchase history, loyalty program activity
- Photos or CCTV footage showing identifiable people
- Employee records (pay, performance, medical notes, leave reasons)

## The core rules

1. **Collect only what you need.** Don't ask for or record information you don't need to complete the task in front of you.
2. **Access only what your role requires.** Looking up a customer's or colleague's record out of curiosity is a violation, even if you never share it.
3. **Never share personal data outside approved channels.** No forwarding customer lists to personal email, no photographing screens with customer data, no discussing specific customers' details on social media or with friends/family.
4. **Secure physical and digital records.** Lock screens when you step away. Don't leave printed customer forms, till receipts with full card numbers, or returns paperwork lying around. Shred documents with personal data before disposal — don't bin them.
5. **Report data incidents immediately.** If data is lost, sent to the wrong person, a laptop/phone is lost or stolen, or you suspect a breach, tell your manager immediately. Fast reporting limits harm — delay makes things worse and will be treated more seriously than the mistake itself.
6. **Payment card data is extra sensitive.** Never write down full card numbers, CVV codes, or PINs. Follow your POS system's prompts — don't work around them to "help" a customer faster.
7. **Customers have rights over their data.** If a customer asks what data we hold on them, or asks us to delete it, don't ignore the request — escalate it to your manager/the designated privacy contact so it's handled correctly and on time.

## Common real-world mistakes

- Leaving a customer's order printout on the counter where the next customer can read it.
- Using a customer's phone number to contact them for a personal reason unrelated to their order.
- Taking a photo of a colleague's work schedule (which may reveal leave reasons) and sharing it in a group chat.
- Looking up a friend's or family member's purchase history "just to check" something, when it isn't part of your job.

## What to do if something goes wrong

Tell your manager the same day. Don't try to quietly fix it yourself or hope no one notices — early reporting is what protects customers and the business, and is always viewed far better than a cover-up.
