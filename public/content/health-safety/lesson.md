# Health, Safety & Emergency Procedures

## Why this matters

A store has daily hazards most people stop noticing after a while: wet floors, stock room ladders, delivery pallets, sharp box cutters, hot equipment. Most workplace injuries come from routine hazards handled carelessly, not dramatic one-off events. This module covers the everyday habits that prevent that, plus what to do in a genuine emergency.

## Everyday hazard habits

- **Spills and wet floors:** clean up or cordon off immediately — don't walk past a spill assuming someone else will handle it.
- **Lifting:** bend your knees, keep the load close to your body, ask for help with anything heavy or awkwardly shaped. Don't stack boxes above a safe height for one person to manage.
- **Box cutters/sharp tools:** retract blades when not in use, cut away from your body, store them properly rather than leaving them on counters.
- **Ladders/step stools:** use the right equipment for height — never stand on shelving, chairs, or boxes to reach something.
- **Blocked exits:** fire exits and the paths to them must stay clear at all times, including during busy delivery days.
- **Reporting hazards:** if you spot a hazard you can't fix immediately (a loose shelf bracket, a flickering light near stock), report it — don't just work around it and hope someone else notices.

## Reporting an incident or near-miss

Report every injury, no matter how minor it seems, and every "near-miss" (something that almost caused harm) to your manager. Near-miss reports are some of the most valuable safety information a business has — they reveal a hazard before it hurts someone.

## In an emergency

### Fire
1. Raise the alarm / alert others.
2. Help customers evacuate calmly via the nearest clear exit — don't use elevators.
3. Go to the designated assembly point.
4. Never go back inside for belongings or stock.

### Medical emergency
1. Don't move an injured person unless they're in immediate danger.
2. Call emergency services (or have someone else do it) while you stay with them.
3. Alert a manager and anyone trained in first aid.

### Robbery or threatening situation
1. Your safety and your colleagues'/customers' safety comes first — comply, don't resist, don't play hero.
2. Don't chase anyone leaving the store.
3. Once safe, alert authorities and your manager, and preserve the scene (don't tidy up) until told it's okay to do so.

## The one habit that matters most

If something feels unsafe, stop and get help before continuing — a delayed task is always better than a preventable injury.
