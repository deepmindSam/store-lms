# IT Security & Password Hygiene

## Why this matters

A single weak password or careless click can expose customer data, lock the store out of its own till system, or let someone commit fraud under your name. Most security incidents don't come from clever hackers — they come from shared passwords, phishing emails, and unlocked screens.

## Password basics

1. **Never share your login** — not with a colleague, not with your manager, not even to "cover" a shift. Every action taken under your account is attributed to you.
2. **Use a unique password for work systems.** Don't reuse your email or social media password for the till system or training portal.
3. **Make it long, not just "complex."** A longer phrase (e.g. three random words plus a number) is stronger and easier to remember than `P@ssw0rd1`.
4. **Never write passwords on sticky notes near a screen or keyboard.**
5. **Change your password immediately** if you think someone else knows it, and tell your manager.

## Recognizing phishing

Phishing is a fake message designed to trick you into giving up a password or clicking a malicious link. Warning signs:

- Urgent language ("Your account will be locked in 1 hour — click here now")
- Requests for your password or payment details via email/text — legitimate systems never ask for your password this way
- Links where the actual address (hover over it) doesn't match the company it claims to be from
- Unexpected attachments, especially from unknown senders

**If in doubt, don't click.** Report suspicious messages to your manager or IT contact instead of forwarding them to colleagues.

## Day-to-day habits

- **Lock your screen** every time you step away from a till or computer, even for a minute.
- **Don't plug in unknown USB drives** found lying around — a classic way malware spreads.
- **Only install software/apps approved for work devices.**
- **Keep work data on work systems** — don't email customer spreadsheets to your personal email "to work on later."

## If something goes wrong

If you clicked a suspicious link, lost a work device, or think your account has been compromised, tell your manager or IT contact immediately. Acting fast limits the damage — waiting to "see if it's actually a problem" is how small incidents become big ones.
