# Leave, Attendance & Scheduling Policy

> **Customize the specifics** (how many days, request lead time, approval system) to match your actual policy — the structure and reasoning below is designed to carry over regardless.

## Why we have a clear process

Unclear or inconsistent leave handling is one of the fastest ways to damage trust in a team — if one person's request gets approved informally while another's gets stuck, people reasonably feel it's unfair. A clear, consistently applied process protects everyone, including you: it means your request is judged on the same rules as everyone else's, not on who happens to be asking.

## Requesting planned leave (holiday, personal time)

1. **Submit requests as early as possible** — the lead time your store requires exists so shifts can be covered properly, not to make it harder to get time off.
2. **Check for blackout periods** (peak trading dates, understaffed weeks) before requesting, where your store has them.
3. **Wait for approval before making firm plans** — a submitted request isn't an approved one until you hear back.
4. **First-come, first-served and fairness matter** — if multiple people want the same dates, decisions follow the store's stated rule (e.g. request order, rotation), not favoritism.

## Sick leave and unplanned absence

1. **Notify your manager as early as possible**, following your store's required method and notice window — don't just not show up and explain later.
2. **Follow any documentation requirements** (e.g. a note for extended absence) your store sets.
3. **Keep your manager updated** if an absence is extending longer than first expected.

## Attendance and punctuality

- Arrive ready to start on time, not arriving at start time and then getting ready.
- If you're going to be late, let your manager know as soon as you know — a heads-up changes a problem into a minor adjustment.
- Repeated lateness or absence, even in small increments, affects the whole team's workload and should be raised directly with your manager rather than left unaddressed.

## Scheduling fairness

- Shift preferences are considered where possible but aren't guaranteed — the schedule balances everyone's needs against the store's coverage requirements.
- Swapping shifts with a colleague is usually fine but must be approved by a manager first, not just agreed between the two of you — the business still needs to know who is actually responsible for a shift.
- Scheduling decisions (who gets preferred shifts, weekends off, etc.) should be applied consistently — if you ever feel a pattern is unfair, raise it rather than assuming nothing can change.

## The underlying principle

Leave and attendance policy isn't about distrust — it's about making sure the store can run reliably for customers and colleagues while still giving everyone fair, predictable access to time off.
