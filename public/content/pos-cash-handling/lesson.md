# POS & Cash Handling Procedures

## Starting your till

1. Count the opening float before your first transaction and confirm it matches the expected amount.
2. Report any discrepancy to a manager before you start serving — once transactions begin, it's much harder to trace the source of a mismatch.
3. Log in under your own account. Never operate under someone else's login.

## During a sale

- **Scan, don't key in, whenever possible** — manual entry is where price errors and fraud both tend to happen.
- **Count change back to the customer out loud**, starting from the item total up to the amount tendered. This protects both you and the customer from disputes.
- **Large notes:** follow your store's counterfeit-check procedure for every large note, every time — not just when something "feels off."
- **Voids and corrections:** if you make a scanning error, void the line properly through the system rather than adjusting cash to compensate. Every void should be traceable to what actually happened.
- **Never leave the till drawer open** between transactions.

## Refunds and discounts

- Refunds follow the validation rules in the *Validation Rules Per Store* module — proof of purchase, condition checks, and approval thresholds apply every time, including for colleagues and friends.
- Discounts beyond standard promotions require the approval level set by your store — don't apply "just this once" exceptions on your own authority.

## Handling cash safely

- Keep large-denomination notes below the till tray, not visible to customers walking past.
- Follow your store's safe/cash-office drop procedure for excess cash — don't let the till build up past the threshold your store sets.
- Never count cash in view of the shop floor or a queue.

## Closing your till

1. Count down the drawer before entering the counted amount into the system — never enter an expected number and adjust the physical count to match.
2. Report variances honestly, even small ones repeated over time — a small variance every shift is a pattern worth investigating, not a rounding error to shrug off.
3. Secure cash according to your store's end-of-day procedure before leaving your till unattended.

## Red flags to report to a manager

- A customer pressuring you to skip a verification step "because they're in a hurry."
- Repeated small variances on the same till or same shift pattern.
- Anyone asking to use your login instead of their own.
