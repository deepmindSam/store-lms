# Store Opening & Closing Checklist

> **Customize the checklist items** in this file to match your actual store layout, systems, and local requirements — the structure and reasoning below should stay useful regardless.

## Why a checklist, not memory

Opening and closing are when the most things can go wrong unnoticed — alarms left disarmed, fridges left unlocked, cash left exposed, systems left logged in. A checklist exists because memory fails under time pressure, especially when you're rushing to open on time or get home at the end of a long shift. Following it every time, not just when you "feel like something's off," is what prevents incidents.

## Opening (example structure — customize per store)

1. **Security:** disarm alarm, check for signs of forced entry or anything disturbed overnight; report anything unusual to a manager before proceeding.
2. **Safety walk:** check fire exits are clear, no spills or hazards on the floor, emergency lighting/signage intact.
3. **Systems:** power on tills and back-office systems, confirm network/payment connectivity before the first customer.
4. **Cash:** count and verify opening floats against expected amounts.
5. **Stock & presentation:** check overnight deliveries are logged, perishables/temperature-sensitive stock checked, shelves/displays presentable.
6. **Staffing:** confirm who's on shift matches the schedule; note any gaps to escalate.

## Closing (example structure — customize per store)

1. **Cash:** count down all tills, reconcile against system totals, secure cash per your store's end-of-day procedure.
2. **Customers:** ensure the store is fully clear before locking up — check fitting rooms, restrooms, and back areas.
3. **Systems:** log off all terminals properly; don't just walk away while still logged in.
4. **Stock & equipment:** store sensitive/high-value stock securely, turn off equipment that should be off overnight, confirm fridges/freezers are closed and functioning.
5. **Safety:** check all fire exits, turn off non-essential lighting per policy, set alarm.
6. **Security:** lock all doors/windows, confirm with a second person where your store requires a two-person close.

## If something doesn't check out

Don't skip ahead and hope it resolves itself — an alarm that won't set, a till that won't balance, a door that won't lock properly are all reasons to stop and call for help (a manager, a locksmith, your store's support line) rather than leaving the premises in an uncertain state.
