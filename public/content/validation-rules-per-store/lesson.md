## Why validation rules exist

Validation rules are the checks that catch errors *before* they become a loss, a safety issue, or an unhappy customer — a price that doesn't match the shelf, a quantity that doesn't match the order, a return that doesn't match policy. Skipping a validation step to save ten seconds is how shrinkage, mis-picks, and compliance breaches happen.

## Categories to define per store *(replace with your actual rules)*

### 1. Pricing & promotions
- How to verify a scanned price matches the shelf/ticketed price before explaining a discrepancy to a customer.
- Who can override a price, and what approval is required.
- How promotional pricing is validated at the start/end of a promotion period.

### 2. Stock & receiving
- What to check when a delivery arrives (quantity vs. purchase order, damage, expiry dates, correct SKU).
- What to do when a count doesn't match the system — who to tell, how to log it.
- Rules for accepting or rejecting a delivery.

### 3. Returns & refunds
- What proof of purchase is required.
- Condition checks before accepting a return.
- Value/approval thresholds that require manager sign-off.

### 4. Age-restricted or regulated items *(if applicable to this store)*
- ID checking procedure and acceptable forms of ID.
- What to do if ID is uncertain or unavailable.

### 5. Cash & till reconciliation
- End-of-shift/end-of-day counting and sign-off procedure.
- Acceptable variance thresholds and escalation steps if exceeded.

## The principle behind every rule

If a validation step feels like it's "just paperwork," ask what it's protecting against before skipping it — usually it's either customer safety, stock accuracy, or financial accuracy. If a rule genuinely no longer makes sense, raise it with your manager so it can be updated for everyone, rather than quietly ignoring it.
